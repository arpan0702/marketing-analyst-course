Resume, LinkedIn & When to Apply

The technical skills only matter if a resume and a LinkedIn profile get someone to look at them. This page covers the rewrite, the keywords, who to target, and exactly when to start.

When to start applying

Week 12 — not "when everything feels ready." By week 12 you'll have Portfolio Projects 1 and 2 published and a rewritten resume; that's enough to start getting screens while Module 5 and Project 3 finish in parallel. See the full picture on the Roadmap.

Translate the resume: skill map

Every "soft" marketing skill has a hard analytical shadow. Name it — don't discard the marketing experience, reframe it.

She already does thisWhich is actually this analytical skill
Writes campaign briefs with a target audience and goal→Defining a hypothesis and success metric before pulling data
Segments audiences for messaging→Customer segmentation — the same operation SQL GROUP BY does
Maps the customer journey funnel→Funnel analysis — conversion rate at each stage, drop-off diagnosis
A/B tests subject lines or creative→Hypothesis testing — already running experiments, just without significance math
Justifies budget to a CMO→ROI / attribution reasoning — the exact skill CAC and ROAS formalize

ATS keyword pass

Resume and LinkedIn "Skills" section should contain, verbatim: SQL, Excel, Google Sheets, GA4, Power BI (add Tableau too only if a specific JD asks for it), A/B testing, data visualization, campaign analytics, CAC, ROAS, LTV. ATS systems match strings, not synonyms — "spreadsheet software" will not match a filter for "Excel."

LinkedIn headline: "Marketing Manager → Marketing Analyst | SQL, GA4, Power BI | Turning campaign data into revenue decisions." The arrow signals "in transition, actively upskilled," not a mismatch between title and target.

Target roles & titles

Search all of these — titles are inconsistent across companies, and narrowing to only "Marketing Analyst" cuts the funnel by more than half.

  • Marketing Analyst
  • Growth Analyst / Growth Marketing Analyst
  • Campaign Performance Analyst
  • Customer Insights Analyst
  • Digital Analytics Specialist / Analyst
  • Marketing Data Analyst
  • CRM Analyst
  • Web Analyst

Application strategy

~20% cold applications, ~60% warm networking/referrals, ~20% recruiter outreach. Referred candidates get interviewed at multiples the rate of cold applicants — for a career-changer without the "safe" title match, a referral is often what gets the resume read at all instead of filtered by an ATS keyword match.

Cold outreach templates

To a recruiter

Hi [Name] — I saw you recruit for marketing/growth analytics roles at [Company]. I'm a Marketing Manager transitioning into Marketing/Growth Analyst work — I've spent [X years] running campaigns and reading performance data, and I've spent the last few months building hands-on SQL, GA4, and dashboarding skills to formalize that into an analyst skillset (happy to share a portfolio piece on campaign ROI analysis). Would love to be on your radar for analyst-track roles you're working — open to a quick chat if it's useful, no pressure either way.

To a Senior Marketing Analyst

Hi [Name] — I came across your profile and saw you made a similar move into analytics from a more marketing-generalist background. I'm a Marketing Manager currently building out SQL/GA4/dashboard skills to move into a Marketing Analyst role, and I'd genuinely value 15 minutes of your perspective — mainly: what actually mattered in your first 90 days that a course wouldn't have taught you? Completely understand if time's tight — even a two-line reply would help.
